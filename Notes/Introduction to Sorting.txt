INTRODUCTION TO SORTING

Sorting is the process of arranging data in a specific order such as ascending or descending order.

Examples:
• Alphabetical order of names
• Numerical order of marks
• Dates and timestamps
• Employee records

Purpose:
• Easier searching
• Better organization
• Faster data retrieval
• Improved data analysis

------------------------------------------------------------
WHY SORTING IS IMPORTANT

Sorting organizes data so that operations such as searching and analysis become more efficient.

Examples:
Unsorted Data: 45 12 89 3 21
Sorted Data: 3 12 21 45 89

Benefits:
• Faster searching
• Easier processing
• Better performance of algorithms
• Improved readability

------------------------------------------------------------
ORDER OF SORTING

Sorting can be performed according to different criteria.

Common Orders:
Ascending Order: 1 2 3 4 5
Descending Order: 5 4 3 2 1
Alphabetical Order:
Apple
Banana
Orange

FACTORS AFFECTING THE CHOICE OF SORTING ALGORITHM

Different sorting algorithms are chosen based on the nature of the data.

Factors:
• Data size
• Memory availability
• Required speed
• Stability requirements
• Complexity of implementation

Several sorting algorithms are available, each with its own advantages.

Examples:

------------------------------------------------------------
BUBBLE SORT

Bubble Sort repeatedly compares adjacent elements and swaps them if they are in the wrong order.
Idea: The largest element "bubbles up" to its correct position after each pass.

Example:
13 12 18 6 -4 9

After Sorting: 
-4 6 9 12 13 18

Properties:
• Simple to understand
• Easy to implement
• Repeatedly swaps adjacent elements

------------------------------------------------------------
WORKING OF BUBBLE SORT

Pass 1:
Compare adjacent elements.
Move the largest element to the last position.

Pass 2:
Ignore the last sorted element.
Move the next largest element to its correct position.

Continue until all elements are sorted.

------------------------------------------------------------
BUBBLE SORT ALGORITHM

1. Start from the first element.
2. Compare adjacent elements.
3. Swap if left element is larger.
4. Continue until the end of the list.
5. Repeat for remaining unsorted elements.
6. Stop when the list becomes sorted.

------------------------------------------------------------
BUBBLE SORT EXAMPLE

Initial List:
13 12 18 6 -4 9

Pass 1 Result:
12 13 6 -4 9 18

Pass 2 Result:
12 6 -4 9 13 18

Pass 3 Result:
6 -4 9 12 13 18

Pass 4 Result:
-4 6 9 12 13 18

Sorted List:
-4 6 9 12 13 18

------------------------------------------------------------
ADVANTAGES OF BUBBLE SORT

• Very simple
• Easy to implement
• Good for small datasets

------------------------------------------------------------
DISADVANTAGES OF BUBBLE SORT

• Slow for large datasets
• Performs many comparisons
• Inefficient compared to advanced sorting methods

------------------------------------------------------------
INSERTION SORT

Insertion Sort builds a sorted portion of the list one element at a time.
Idea: Each new element is inserted into its correct position inside the sorted part of the list.

Example:
13 12 18 6 -4 9

After Sorting:
-4 6 9 12 13 18

Properties:
• Maintains a sorted and unsorted section.
• Inserts elements into proper positions.
• Similar to arranging playing cards.

------------------------------------------------------------
WORKING OF INSERTION SORT

Initial: 13 | 12 18 6 -4 9
Sorted Portion: 13

Insert 12:
12 13 | 18 6 -4 9

Insert 18:
12 13 18 | 6 -4 9

Insert 6:
6 12 13 18 | -4 9

Insert -4:
-4 6 12 13 18 | 9

Insert 9:
-4 6 9 12 13 18

------------------------------------------------------------
INSERTION SORT ALGORITHM

1. Assume first element is sorted.
2. Select the next element.
3. Compare it with elements in the sorted section.
4. Shift larger elements to the right.
5. Insert element at correct position.
6. Repeat until all elements are processed.

------------------------------------------------------------
INSERTION SORT EXAMPLE

Initial List: 13 12 18 6 -4 9

Pass 1: 12 13 18 6 -4 9
Pass 2: 12 13 18 6 -4 9
Pass 3: 6 12 13 18 -4 9
Pass 4: -4 6 12 13 18 9
Pass 5: -4 6 9 12 13 18

Sorted List: -4 6 9 12 13 18

------------------------------------------------------------
ADVANTAGES OF INSERTION SORT

• Simple implementation
• Efficient for small datasets
• Good for nearly sorted data
• Requires little extra memory

------------------------------------------------------------
DISADVANTAGES OF INSERTION SORT

• Slow for large datasets
• Performance decreases as data size increases

------------------------------------------------------------
SELECTION SORT

Selection Sort repeatedly finds the smallest element from the unsorted part and places it in the correct position.
Idea: Select the minimum element and move it to the beginning.

Example:
13 12 18 6 -4 9

After Sorting:
-4 6 9 12 13 18

Properties:
• Divides list into sorted and unsorted sections.
• Performs fewer swaps than Bubble Sort.

------------------------------------------------------------
WORKING OF SELECTION SORT

Initial: 13 12 18 6 -4 9

Pass 1: Select smallest element (-4).
-4 12 18 6 13 9

Pass 2: Select next smallest element (6).
-4 6 18 12 13 9

Pass 3: Select next smallest element (9).
-4 6 9 12 13 18

Continue until completed.

------------------------------------------------------------
SELECTION SORT ALGORITHM

1. Find the smallest element.
2. Swap it with the first unsorted position.
3. Move boundary of sorted section one step right.
4. Repeat until all elements are sorted.

------------------------------------------------------------
SELECTION SORT EXAMPLE

Initial List: 13 12 18 6 -4 9

Pass 1: -4 12 18 6 13 9
Pass 2: -4 6 18 12 13 9
Pass 3: -4 6 9 12 13 18
Pass 4: -4 6 9 12 13 18
Pass 5: -4 6 9 12 13 18

Sorted List: -4 6 9 12 13 18

------------------------------------------------------------
ADVANTAGES OF SELECTION SORT

• Easy to understand
• Performs fewer swaps
• Requires little extra memory

------------------------------------------------------------
DISADVANTAGES OF SELECTION SORT

• Slow for large datasets
• Always performs many comparisons even when data is nearly sorted

------------------------------------------------------------
COMPARISON OF BUBBLE SORT, INSERTION SORT AND SELECTION SORT

Bubble Sort:
• Compares adjacent elements
• Largest element moves to the end
• Many swaps

Insertion Sort:
• Inserts element into sorted portion
• Efficient for nearly sorted data
• Fewer comparisons in partially sorted lists

Selection Sort:
• Selects minimum element
• Fewer swaps
• Same number of comparisons regardless of initial order

------------------------------------------------------------
TIME COMPLEXITY

Bubble Sort:
Best Case    : O(n)
Average Case : O(n²)
Worst Case   : O(n²)

Insertion Sort:
Best Case    : O(n)
Average Case : O(n²)
Worst Case   : O(n²)

Selection Sort:
Best Case    : O(n²)
Average Case : O(n²)
Worst Case   : O(n²)
